The film they can’t findThe film they can’t find

In the 1990s, if you thought a friend should see a film, you lent them the tape.

Now I tell a friend about a film, they look for it, and it isn’t on anything they can stream. Even when it’s mine to share, sending it from my media server means an account for the sake of one film, a way into my home network, and hoping my upload speed holds up. So I tell them again the next time I see them, and they still haven’t seen it.

Open Matte is the tape.

How it worksHow it works

  1. 1Put it on the shelf

    Prepare a film once on your own computer, and copy it to your server.

  2. 2Write the staff pick

    Each film has its own page, with the poster, the details, and your few lines on why it’s worth their evening.

  3. 3Lend a tape

    A tape is a link that opens the film in your friend’s browser. They don’t need an app or a password, and you don’t need to make them an account.

Due backDue back

Anyone with the link can play the tape. To lend a tape to one person only, give them a membership card. They sign in with a passkey, not a password.

You can limit the number of plays, set a date to start by, or allow a number of days after the first play. Take a tape back and it stops working almost straight away.

Pressing Play starts the clock. Your friend can pause and come back within 48 hours by default, on one device at a time. The picture adjusts to their connection, up to 4K HDR on screens that can show it, and they choose their audio and subtitles. Nobody has to rewind.

What it won’t doWhat it won’t do

Open Matte isn’t a media server. There’s no library of everything you own, and the server never converts a film.

It doesn’t use DRM (copy protection), so anyone who can play a film could record it. The aim is private enough for friends.

It’s less than a media server and, for this one job, better.

Only share films you have the right to share.

What you needWhat you need

Setting up Open Matte takes some work. After that, lending a film is as casual as sending any other link.

Yours
  • A Linux server with Docker, a public IPv4 address, and ports 80 and 443 open
  • A domain
  • Films you prepare on your own computer, with Node and ffmpeg
  • Disk space: up to about 38 GB for a two-hour 4K HDR film, often much less
  • A TMDB key, if you want details and artwork
Theirs
  • A browser
  • A membership card, only if you give them one

Put Open Matte on a server away from home and your friends never touch your home network. Your home upload speed only matters when you copy a film across.
